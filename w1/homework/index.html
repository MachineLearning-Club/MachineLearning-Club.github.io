Choose an appropriate AI approach for real problems, then practise elementary probability calculations.

Some situations allow more than one valid approach; explain your choice in one sentence.

Homework (2p) — Compute probabilities

Show the sample space, relevant event counts or probabilities, your calculation, and a final answer. A calculator is allowed.

1. A ten-sided die

Roll a fair ten-sided die. Let X be the face it shows.

  1. Calculate P(X = 4).
  2. Calculate P(X is even).
  3. Given that X is even, find P(X = 4 | X is even).
  4. Explain why your answers to (a) and (c) differ.
Practice on sample spaces and conditional probability from the Week 1 course.

2. Two objects from a bag

A bag contains 3 red and 2 blue tokens.

  1. If a token is replaced after the first draw, what is the probability of drawing two red tokens?
  2. If it is not replaced, what is the probability of drawing two red tokens?
  3. Why are the answers different?
New practice problem on independence and conditional probability.

3. Did the student know the answer?

A student has a 60% chance of knowing a true/false answer. Otherwise they guess, with a 50% chance of answering correctly. You observe a correct answer.

  1. Find P(correct).
  2. Use Bayes' rule to find P(knew | correct).
  3. Why is the result not 100%?
Numeric adaptation of the provided probability handout, worked exercise 7.
