Week 01 · Laboratory

AI Tools — Exploration Laboratory

Explore several real AI systems, compare what they do, and record where they succeed or fail. No coding required.

Laboratory — explore AI in practice

Work individually or in pairs. Try a drawing recogniser, train a small image classifier, then compare a generative assistant with another AI system. Your goal is to observe the different tasks, not just click through demos.

Before you start

Use safe examples

Draw simple objects and use ordinary classroom objects such as a pen, ruler, bottle or notebook. Do not upload classmates' faces, voices, personal messages, or private information. Accounts and cameras may be optional; ask the teacher before signing into third-party services.

Record observations in a text document or on paper. If a website is unavailable or blocked, use another activity below and record the reason.

1. Quick, Draw! — an AI recognises sketches

GOOGLE · COMPUTER VISION
Quick, Draw!Open Quick, Draw!
  1. Play at least five rounds. Draw the prompted objects as normally as you can.
  2. Record two correct guesses and one unsuccessful or late guess.
  3. Explain why a recogniser may fail even when the drawing is understandable to a person.
Observation

The tool predicts categories from a drawing. This is an example of computer vision and classification.

2. Teachable Machine — train your own classifier

GOOGLE · SUPERVISED LEARNING
Teachable MachineOpen Teachable Machine
  1. Choose an Image Project with two classes, for example Pen and Notebook.
  2. Provide around 10 varied examples for each class using ordinary objects. Change the angle, position or background.
  3. Select Train Model and test at least four fresh examples.
  4. Record the predicted category, whether it was correct, and any displayed prediction scores.

3. Generative AI — test a claim, not just a response

GOOGLE · GENERATIVE AI
GeminiOpen Gemini (if available)

Use this non-personal prompt with a generative assistant:

“Explain in 80 words how a neural network differs from a rule-based program. Give one real example of each.”
  1. Record the two examples the assistant gives.
  2. Check one claim against the course definitions or another reliable source.
  3. Ask a follow-up that requests a clearer or shorter explanation. Did the answer become more useful?
Observation

A fluent answer is not proof of correctness. A generative model produces content; it does not necessarily verify every statement it makes.
