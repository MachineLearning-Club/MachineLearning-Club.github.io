What Is AI? Rules and Learning

Problem 42 — Va întârzia avionul? A laboratory on reading training data and selecting the first ID3 split.

Lab challenge

Imagine you work with an airline and need a first decision rule for predicting flight delays. You have summaries from 80 flights, but not the complete individual-flight records. Can you choose the best question to ask first?

Learning objectives

Read a contingency table, compare information gain across attributes, build a one-question decision tree (decision stump), and identify what the summary table cannot tell us.

1. Inspect the flight observations

Each weather or time attribute is considered separately. Under each value, count how many flights were delayed and not delayed. These numbers are taken from Problem 42 in the supplied ID3 collection.

Observation

The table contains six separate two-way summaries of the same 80 flights. A row does not identify an individual flight or tell us whether, for example, rainy flights were also in summer.

Exercise 1
1)
  1. Which attribute produces the highest information gain? What does that tell you about the quality of its two groups?
  2. Identify one attribute that gives no information about delay status.
Show a possible solution

a) Summer has the largest gain (approximately 0.4564 bits). Its two groups are strongly skewed toward opposite outcomes. b) Daytime: both branches contain exactly 20 delayed and 20 not-delayed flights, giving zero information gain.

2)
  1. Explain why the Summer = Yes branch predicts Not delayed.
  2. How many of the 80 recorded flights does this one-question tree misclassify?
Show a possible solution

a) Among summer flights, 35 were not delayed and only 5 were delayed. b) Ten flights: five errors in the Yes branch and five in the No branch.

4. Can we choose a second-level attribute?

Observation

The table only shows separate counts for Rain, Wind, Summer, Winter, Daytime and Nighttime. It does not tell us how attributes occur together. To choose the second split under Summer = Yes, we would need counts such as rainy summer flights delayed / not delayed.

Exercise 3
3)
  1. Why is it impossible to determine the best second-level attribute from this summary table alone?
  2. Give one joint count that would help you decide how to split the Summer = Yes branch.
Show a possible solution

a) Information gain at level two requires conditional counts within each Summer branch. The marginal counts in the table do not supply them. b) For example, the number of flights with Summer = Yes and Rain = Yes that were delayed, and the corresponding not-delayed number.

Exit ticket

In two sentences, explain why Summer is a useful first question and why the available table is insufficient to construct the complete ID3 tree.
