Week 09 · Homework

Mid-Term Evaluation

Situation — after the science-fair demonstration

Your team has finished the Week 9 practical. A visitor notices that the recogniser sometimes confuses similar handwritten digits. The team must submit a short technical follow-up that corrects the description of its accuracy and investigates one change.

What you already have: the same 600/200 labelled split and a reproducible 3-nearest-neighbour notebook. Its default held-out result is 196/200 (98%). That number does not describe every handwriting style.

Mandatory (1p) — communicate accurately

Rewrite the following unsupported sentence:

“Our AI reads all handwritten numbers with 98% certainty.”

Your revision must include (a) the tested set size, (b) the model/settings used, and (c) one limitation. Do not change the measured numerator to make your report sound better.

One defensible example

“Our 3-nearest-neighbour model recognised 196 of 200 previously withheld 8×8 digit images in a fixed classroom subset. Its performance on other handwriting, cameras and image formats has not yet been established.”

Homework (2p) — controlled comparison

  1. Open your Week 9 practical notebook. Preserve the original baseline result for k=3.
  2. Change only K to 5; rerun evaluation on the same 200 original held-out examples.
  3. Make a small table with model setting, correct count, wrong count and accuracy; each metric must use the correct denominator.
  4. Locate one example with a mistake; give its actual digit, predicted digit and a brief visual reason why confusion might be plausible.
  5. Explain what evidence would justify using one model setting rather than the other and why repeated test-score tuning needs separate validation.

Minimum accepted deliverable: the executable notebook and six to ten lines of explanation. A screenshot without the run-ready code is not enough to reproduce the experiment.

Research (3p) — optional

Construct a new training/validation/final-test split from the available labels, taking care not to mix image indices. Choose k based on validation accuracy, then evaluate exactly once on the separate final test set. Compare this with choosing k directly from the displayed 200-image classroom test set. Explain which approach supports a stronger final claim.

Submission

Use the Week 9 laboratory ZIP preparation. Include digits-practical.ipynb, the comparison table and your revised public claim. The local ZIP does not upload itself.

Bibliography

  • Week 8: evaluation and limitations; Week 9: evidence chain and rubric.
  • Alpaydin & Kaynak (1998), UCI Optical Recognition of Handwritten Digits.
