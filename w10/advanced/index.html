Week 10 · Advanced

Generative AI: GPT, Data and Society

Reasoning problems — optional

These are not required for the first encounter with generative AI. Try each short problem before revealing the solution.

Three worked challenges

1. Illustrative token probabilities

A toy model assigns candidate probabilities 0.6, 0.3 and 0.1. What is their sum? What is the probability of choosing anything other than the first candidate?

Solution

Sum is 1; the other two together have probability 0.3+0.1=0.4. The numbers are invented and do not describe an actual GPT model's output.

2. An incorrect claim sounds confident

A generated answer reports that 15 of 24 pupils voted yes, and calls that 75%. Check it.

Solution

15÷24×100=62.5%, not 75%. The count can be used if it comes from the actual anonymous tally; the model's percentage must be corrected.

3. An apparent preference changes the feed

A toy feed ranks by click counts. First: sports=5 and music=1. After three additional music clicks, which ranks first? Explain why this is a limited model of behaviour.

Solution

Sports still leads 5 to 4. Clicking can express curiosity, surprise or disagreement, so count is not a complete description of preference or identity.

Beyond this lesson

University-level source materials contain API protocols, structured schemas, agent loops, orchestration and safety controls. We use them as teacher background only. A useful student takeaway is simpler: know what was observed, what was predicted and what was independently checked.
