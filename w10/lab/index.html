1. What does a language model read?

Enter a sentence with punctuation and use the classroom toy splitter below. Our splitter illustrates sequences; it is not the real tokenizer used by any GPT model.

Task: predict the pieces for AI can help, but verify!, then compare. Explain why real models may split words differently.

Explore Transformer Explainer ↗ as an optional visual example; do not attempt to calculate attention matrices.

2. A feed follows interactions, not thoughts

Use fake click counts and change which recommendation comes first.

  1. Find settings that make music first.
  2. Find settings where both subjects are equally ranked.
  3. Explain one reason the ranking can be unfair or misleading.

3. Run a prepared Python notebook in your browser

It contains a word-count demonstration and a toy recommendation ranking. No accounts, cameras, API keys or student data are used.

Predict the output, then run.

Open JupyterLite (no account) ↗ · Download the Week 10 notebook · How to upload and run it

Change one thing: make music the top category; record the original and updated outputs.

4. Check a generated newsletter sentence

An imagined draft claims “80% of pupils voted yes.” The fictional tally is 12 yes and 8 no. Work out the true percentage and record claim / evidence / verdict. This is verification—not a contest to make the AI sound convincing.

Reveal the check

12÷(12+8)=12÷20=60%. The claimed 80% does not match the data. The newsletter must be corrected before publication.

Research (3p) — optional

Write a Python function percentage(yes, no). Handle the case yes+no=0 without dividing by zero. Record two successful tests and one empty-data test.

Submission

One completed notebook or short document containing a token sequence, two feed rankings and the corrected vote claim. Use invented or anonymous data only. Your laboratory submission controls prepare a local download; they do not send files automatically.
